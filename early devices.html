HISTORY OF COMPUTERS

A brief history of the evolution of computers

Early Calculating Inventions

Abacus (c. 2700 BCE)

The abacus is a simple counting device that has been used for thousands of years. It consists of a frame with rods or wires, on which beads are moved to perform arithmetic calculations. The abacus was widely used in ancient civilizations, including China, Mesopotamia, and Egypt.

Napier's Bones (1617)

Napier's bones, invented by John Napier in the early 17th century, are a set of numbered rods that can be used to perform multiplication and division. Each rod is marked with a series of numbers that represent multiples of a particular digit.

The Pascaline (1642)

The Pascaline, invented by Blaise Pascal in 1642, is a mechanical calculator that can perform addition and subtraction. It uses a series of wheels and gears to carry out calculations.

Analytical Engine & Ada Lovelace (1815-1852)

The Analytical Engine, designed by Charles Babbage in the 19th century, was a proposed mechanical general-purpose computer. Ada Lovelace, a mathematician, is often credited with writing the first algorithm intended for processing by the Analytical Engine.
